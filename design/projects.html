Interpretable deep learning: heatmaps and Kolmogorov–Arnold networks

Research on the black-box problem in formal decision-making, in a team with lawyers. I built a Python tool that computes neural-network heatmaps and metrics on them, reproducing results from a paper, and I research how Kolmogorov–Arnold networks compare with MLPs in performance and interpretability. The work is aimed at a paper for the journal.

Motor insurance pricing: from GLMs to XGBoost

A team project on a real motor insurance dataset of about 100,000 rows: data quality checks, missing values, new features, and removing features that leaked future information. We modeled claim frequency and severity and compared GLMs with tree-based methods, linear regression and neural networks. I'm now rebuilding it in Python with gradient boosting and a FastAPI service in Docker.

DeepAR from scratch

An implementation of Amazon's DeepAR on top of PyTorch's LSTM, applied to stock market time series. For every step the model outputs a predictive distribution instead of a single number. I benchmarked it against AR, ARIMA, GARCH and a random walk on RMSE, ND and the 10% and 90% quantile losses.

A/B agent

It started as a Python toolkit for A/B testing and causal inference: z-tests, power and sample size, multiple-testing corrections, difference-in-differences and synthetic control, with CUPED variance reduction in progress. With a MATF classmate I am turning it into an agent that runs the whole analysis from a dataset and a question.
